# The professional standard — what TJR and the top Whop operators actually do

Researched live 2026-08-16 against TJR Trades, brezscales, and the Whop trading cohort.
This is the bar. Everything in the rebuild is measured against it.

---

## The headline finding

**The operators at this tier are not less careful than us about claims. They are far more
aggressive about everything else.** TJR's own risk disclaimer sits in the footer, once, in
small text. What's above it is dense, confident, and repetitive in the way sales pages are
supposed to be.

Our mistake was not that we removed the income figures. It's that we replaced them with
*hedging*, and put the hedging everywhere. TJR discloses in one place and sells in twelve.
We were disclosing in nine and selling in two.

**The fix is not more claims. It's more selling around one clean disclosure.**

## Register: how confidence is projected

TJR's hero, verbatim:

> **TJR Trades: From $0 to 7-Figure Trader**
> Join the TJR Trading Bootcamp — the #1 free trading course with 1,614+ five-star reviews.
> *"I'm just like you, but rich."* — TJR

Note what that does: **states a claim about himself, not a promise to you.** "I'm just like
you, but rich" is swagger that carries zero guarantee. It's aggressive and it's survivable,
because the subject is the founder, not the customer's future returns.

**That distinction is the whole game.** Confident copy about *who you are and what you
built* is unlimited. Copy about *what the reader will earn* is the regulated part. We had
this backwards — we hedged the founder's story and then worried about the numbers.

### The register rules we adopt

- **Assert what's true about Torin without qualification.** Nine years. Built rigs before
  he could legally use an exchange. Ran the system through a full cycle. Published every
  reading. No hedge needed on any of that.
- **Hedge once, where it belongs.** One disclosure block per page, at the foot or in a
  single labelled panel. Not in every section.
- **Never apologise for the product mid-pitch.** "Don't trust my backtest" is a great email
  subject and a terrible page heading.
- **Transparency framed as a flex, not a confession.** "Every number recomputable from
  public data — check it yourself" is a challenge. "Here's what's wrong with my data" is an
  apology. Same fact.

9. Email opt-in
10. Testimonials — "Real Traders, Real Results"
11. What's inside

13. Free video library
14. Final CTA

**Fifteen sections. Testimonials twice. CTA at least four times.** Ours had testimonials
zero times and a proof section that argued with itself.

## CTA cadence

TJR's free-course page repeats the primary CTA **three times minimum**, with friction
removers attached every time:

> "No credit card required. Instant access. Lifetime access."

Button copy is imperative and specific: **"Access Free TJR Bootcamp"**, **"Start Free
Course Now"**, **"Enroll Free Now"** — never "Learn more".

## Offer framing and value stacking

> **"Valued at $997 — yours for free"**

Plus six stacked bonuses listed as discrete line items: lifetime access, community,
updates, Q&A support, calculator, journal.

**The mechanic:** name a value, then discount it to the actual price. LiftOffr does none of
this. `/plan` says "$29" and lists features. It never anchors.

## On brezscales — a flag, not a judgment

Bergen Resnik, ~21, sells freelance/e-commerce brand scaling on Whop (Brand Scaling
Bootcamp, Elite Round Table, Scale 2.0), high-ticket up to ~$4,800.

**Two things worth knowing before copying his playbook:**

1. **He's not in this category.** E-commerce/marketing coaching, not trading or crypto.
   The regulatory surface is different — earnings claims about a *business opportunity*
   are the FTC's Business Opportunity Rule territory, which is if anything stricter than
   what applies to LiftOffr.
2. **His programs carry active, public fraud accusations.** Multiple review sites and
   forum threads allege fake success stories and pyramid-scheme structure. No legal
   ruling exists either way. But the specific tactics drawing those accusations are
   exactly the ones we're not doing: unverifiable income testimonials and results claims.

**What's worth taking from him:** production polish, aggressive offer stacking, confident
founder-forward positioning. **What isn't:** the results-claim layer that's generating the
accusations. Copying that is how a business becomes the case study rather than the operator
citing one.
