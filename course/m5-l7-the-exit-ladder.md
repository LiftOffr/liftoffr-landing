# Lesson 7 — Build your own exit ladder

Both entry and exit decisions carry risk. Waiting does not guarantee recovery from a poor entry, and a ladder does not guarantee that an exit will execute.

This lesson gives you the structure and none of the numbers. The numbers are yours — and by now you should understand exactly why nobody who does not know your position size, your timeline and your tax situation can hand them to you honestly.

## 1. What an exit-zone entry actually is

The Score's top band is **85–100**. Two things are true about it at once, and any version of this that tells you only the first is selling you something:

- **Every cycle top since 2013 printed with the Score in that band.** 2013, 2017, 2021, 2025.
- **The Score has also sat in that band for months with no top following.**

So an exit-zone reading is not "the top is in". It is *"the model reads conditions that have been present at every top, and also present at times that were not tops."* That is a genuinely useful thing to know and a genuinely useless thing to treat as a trigger.

## 2. Why a ladder rather than a decision

A single exit is a bet on one date. A ladder is a bet on a range, and the range is the honest shape of the uncertainty.

Exit in one action and you have two ways to be wrong and one to be right: too early, too late, or the day itself. Splitting the exit across several crossings turns *"was I right about the date"* into *"was I roughly right about the region"* — a question the model can actually speak to.

**The tradeoff:** spreading an exit can reduce dependence on one execution date, but can sell early, leave exposure unsold or fail to execute. It guarantees neither a favorable price nor protection against total loss. Fees, taxes and liquidity still matter.

## 3. The whipsaw rule — the part that is actually hard

Between 16 November 2024 and 21 October 2025 the Score entered the exit zone **three times** and dropped back out **three times**. Six transitions in twelve months — **not six signals**. The top was **6 October 2025 at $124,824**, and the model left the zone for the last time **fifteen days after it**. Two of the three entries contained no top at all. The dated log is <#1540313303480275055>.

Any rule of the form "above X, do something" will flip back and forth around X. That is not a broken threshold; it is what thresholds do. So a rule that survives contact with reality has to answer three questions a list of levels does not:

**1. What happens on a crossing?** If the answer is "all of it", six transitions would have meant six reversals in a year. A review-only rule or no transaction can also be a valid choice.

**2. What happens on a retreat?** Most people write down what they will do if the Score *enters* the exit zone and nothing at all about what they will do when it *leaves*. Two of three entries retreated without a top. A rule with no answer here gets abandoned the first time it happens.

**3. Does re-entering the zone a second time mean more than the first?** Decide before you find out, because in the moment it will feel like it does.

**Torin's own answer, as a worked example rather than a recommendation:** a fraction per crossing rather than a decision per crossing, and no reversal on the way back out. That general rule is stated in **section 5b of the $29 Plan document** alongside these same six transitions. Section 5b does not publish numerical exit fractions; it points readers to the full exit method in the Cycle System. This lesson provides a worksheet for choosing your own fractions.

## 4. Why you decide it in the calm

Torin’s published, unaudited account describes giving back roughly **$30,000** through the 2022 bear market. **After the 27 June 2025 crossing**, he began laddering out over the following weeks. **Being early cost him the last leg of the move.** This is his own unaudited account, separate from the historical model record.

A written rule addresses process; it does not establish that a model has predictive value. Evaluate the model and execution separately, including early warnings and missed upside.

A course that teaches its own method's failure mode is the only kind worth paying for. So here it is in full:

> **A ladder distributes intended exits. It does not guarantee fills, recovery, or protection from total loss.**

If that is not the promise you wanted, the refund is on the page and I would rather you took it than stayed on a misunderstanding.

## 5. The worksheet

Work through the worksheet with fictional amounts first. No order placement is required. Review assumptions carefully and revise them when evidence or circumstances change.

```
STEP 1 — WHAT IS ACTUALLY ON THE TABLE

  The position this plan covers .............. ____________________
  What fraction of it is genuinely long-term
  holding you would not sell at any reading? . ____________________

  That fraction is not part of this ladder. Take it out now, before
  you set anything, so you are laddering a number you can act on
  rather than your whole stack.

STEP 2 — HOW MANY RUNGS, AND ON WHAT

  Number of tranches ......................... ______
  Triggered by:  [ ] Score band crossings
                 [ ] price levels
                 [ ] both, whichever comes first

  Fewer rungs = sharper timing risk. More rungs = more of the exit
  happens away from the peak. There is no correct number, and anyone
  who gives you one has not asked what you own.
```

```
STEP 3 — THE RUNGS

  Rung 1 .... fires at __________  fraction of ladder: ______
  Rung 2 .... fires at __________  fraction of ladder: ______
  Rung 3 .... fires at __________  fraction of ladder: ______
  Rung 4 .... fires at __________  fraction of ladder: ______
                             must total .................. 100%

STEP 4 — THE WHIPSAW ANSWER  (do not skip this one)

  If the Score leaves the exit zone after rung 1 fires, I:
    [ ] do nothing and wait for the next crossing
    [ ] re-enter the position I exited
    [ ] something else: ______________________________

  Does a second entry into the zone mean more than the first?
    [ ] yes, and here is how much more: ______________
    [ ] no, the rungs fire identically regardless of sequence

  Between Nov 2024 and Oct 2025 you would have faced this three
  times. Answer it once, now, instead.

STEP 5 — THE FALLBACK

  If no rung has fired by ____________ I will: ______________

  A ladder that can leave you fully exposed through an entire cycle
  is not a plan. The buy-side worksheet has the same step running
  the other way.

STEP 6 — TONIGHT

  [ ] Hypothetical order mechanics and non-fill risks understood
  [ ] Fallback date in my calendar, with an alert
  [ ] Tax questions and costs identified for independent review
  [ ] Told the one other person who needs to know

THE OVERRIDE LOG — write the reason BEFORE you act

  Rung: ______  Date: __________
  Reason: ______________________________________

  Rung: ______  Date: __________
  Reason: ______________________________________
```

Overriding is not forbidden. Overriding **silently** is how a plan quietly stops existing. Writing the reason first turns an impulse into an argument, without claiming that writing guarantees a better outcome.
