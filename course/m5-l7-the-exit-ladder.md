## `#m5-l7-build-your-exit-ladder` — Build your own exit ladder

Buying is the easy half. You can be wrong about an entry and fix it by waiting. You cannot fix a
missed exit by waiting, because the thing you were waiting for already happened.

This lesson gives you the structure and none of the numbers. **The numbers are yours**, and by the
end of Module 5 you should understand exactly why nobody who does not know your position size,
your timeline and your tax situation can give them to you honestly.

### 1. What an exit-zone entry actually is

The Score's top band is **85–100**. Two things are true about it at once, and a version of this
that tells you only the first is selling you something:

- **Every cycle top since 2013 printed with the Score in that band.** 2013, 2017, 2021, 2025.
- **The Score has also sat in that band for months with no top following.**

So an exit-zone reading is not "the top is in". It is "the model reads conditions that have been
present at every top, and also present at times that were not tops". That is a genuinely useful
thing to know and a genuinely useless thing to treat as a trigger.

Everything below follows from holding both halves of that sentence at the same time.

### 2. Why a ladder rather than a decision

A single exit is a bet on one date. A ladder is a bet on a range, and the range is the honest
shape of the uncertainty.

If you exit in one action you have two ways to be wrong and only one to be right: too early, too
late, or the day itself. Splitting the same exit across several crossings converts "was I right
about the date" into "was I roughly right about the region", which is a question the model can
actually speak to.

**The cost is real and worth stating.** A ladder guarantees you will not get the best available
price, because part of your exit happens away from the peak by construction. What you buy with
that is the removal of the outcome where you get none of it. Whether that trade is worth making is
a question about you, not about the model.

### 3. The whipsaw rule — the part that is actually hard

This is the lesson inside the lesson, and it is the one that cost real money to learn.

Between 16 November 2024 and 21 October 2025 the Score entered the exit zone **three times** and
dropped back out **three times**:

```
16 Nov 2024   into exit zone     BTC $90,568
18 Feb 2025   back out           BTC $95,444
 8 May 2025   into exit zone     BTC $103,070
19 Jun 2025   back out           BTC $104,710
27 Jun 2025   into exit zone     BTC $107,091
21 Oct 2025   back out           BTC $108,700
```

Six transitions in twelve months. **Not six signals — three entries and three retreats.** The top
was 6 October 2025 at $124,824, and the model left the zone for the last time fifteen days *after*
it. Two of the three entries contained no top at all.

Any rule of the form "above X, do something" will flip back and forth around X. That is not a
broken threshold; it is what thresholds do. So a rule that survives contact with reality has to
answer three questions that a list of levels does not:

1. **What happens on a crossing?** If the answer is "all of it", six transitions in a year would
   have meant six reversals. If the answer is "nothing", you did not need the model.
2. **What happens on a retreat?** Most people write down what they will do if the Score enters the
   exit zone and nothing at all about what they will do when it leaves. Two of three entries
   retreated without a top. A rule with no answer here gets abandoned the first time it happens.
3. **Does re-entering the zone a second time mean more than the first?** Decide before you find
   out, because in the moment it will feel like it does.

**Torin's own answer, as a worked example rather than a recommendation:** a fraction per crossing
rather than a decision per crossing, and no reversal on the way back out. It is stated in section
5b of the $29 plan document alongside the six transitions above. It is what he does. It is not a
prescription, and the fractions in it are his, chosen against his own position and timeline.

### 4. Why you decide it in the calm

The evidence for this is his, and it is not flattering.

**November 2021.** Every one of these indicators was on the screen and flagging. He did nothing,
and round-tripped roughly **$30,000** through the bear that followed. Three reasons, all common:
ego, because being right had become an identity rather than a position; no written rule, so every
day was a fresh decision made while looking at the price; and "it's going to a million, I'm not
selling here", which cost more than the other two combined.

**And the other direction, October 2025.** He had a rule that time, acted on an early crossing,
and **exited before the top. Being early cost him the last leg of the move.**

Both failures share a root and neither is the model's. In 2021 there was no decision made in
advance. In 2025 there was one, but it had no answer for question 2 above — what a *retreat*
means, and whether the first crossing of three should be treated the same as the last.

A course that teaches its own method's failure mode is the only kind worth paying for. This is
that mode, stated in full: **the ladder does not stop you being early. It stops being early from
costing you everything.**

### 5. The worksheet

Thirty minutes, in one sitting, while nothing is happening. A ladder written across three weeks is
a ladder you renegotiated with yourself twice.

```
STEP 1 — WHAT IS ACTUALLY ON THE TABLE

  The position this plan covers .............. ____________________
  What fraction of it is genuinely long-term
  holding you would not sell at any reading? . ____________________

  That fraction is not part of this ladder. Take it out now, before
  you set anything, so you are laddering a number you can actually
  act on rather than your whole stack.

STEP 2 — HOW MANY RUNGS, AND ON WHAT

  Number of tranches ......................... ______
  Triggered by:  ☐ Score band crossings
                 ☐ price levels
                 ☐ both, whichever comes first

  Fewer rungs = sharper timing risk. More rungs = more of the exit
  happens away from the peak. There is no correct number and anyone
  who gives you one has not asked what you own.

STEP 3 — THE RUNGS

  Rung 1 .... fires at __________  fraction of the ladder: ______
  Rung 2 .... fires at __________  fraction of the ladder: ______
  Rung 3 .... fires at __________  fraction of the ladder: ______
  Rung 4 .... fires at __________  fraction of the ladder: ______
                              must total ..................  100%

STEP 4 — THE WHIPSAW ANSWER (do not skip this one)

  If the Score leaves the exit zone after rung 1 fires, I:
    ☐ do nothing and wait for the next crossing
    ☐ re-enter the position I exited
    ☐ something else: ______________________________________

  Does the second entry into the zone mean more than the first?
    ☐ yes, and here is how much more: ______________________
    ☐ no, the rungs fire identically regardless of sequence

  Between Nov 2024 and Oct 2025 you would have answered this three
  times. Answer it once, now, instead.

STEP 5 — THE FALLBACK

  If no rung has fired by ______________ I will: ______________

  A ladder that can leave you fully exposed through an entire cycle
  is not a plan. The buy-side worksheet has the same step for the
  same reason, running the other way.

STEP 6 — TONIGHT

  ☐ Limit orders placed for every rung that can be pre-placed
  ☐ Fallback date in my calendar with an alert
  ☐ Tax position understood for the size I have just written down
  ☐ Told the one other person who needs to know
```

**The override log.** Same rule as the buy side: if you change or skip a rung, write why *before*
you act.

```
  Rung: ______  Date: __________
  Reason: ______________________________________________
```

Overriding is not forbidden. Overriding *silently* is how a plan quietly stops existing. Writing
the reason first turns an impulse into an argument, and about half of impulses do not survive
being written down.
