## `#m4-l10-context-zero-weight` — What carries zero weight, and why

The Score has nine components. Plenty of well-known Bitcoin indicators are not among them, and
the omissions are deliberate rather than oversights. Four come up often enough to be worth
answering directly.

The distinction that matters throughout: an indicator can be **context** — something worth
looking at — without being an **input**, something the number is built from. Confusing the two is
how people end up unable to reproduce a figure they were told they could check.

### CBBI — the source, not a component

**What it is.** The Colin Talks Crypto Bitcoin Bull Run Index: a composite that averages its own
set of sub-indicators into a single 0–100 reading, published free and daily.

**Why it carries zero weight.** Because it is the *pipe*, not a *component*. The Score reads its
nine component values **from** CBBI's public data. Including CBBI's own composite as a tenth
input would count the same underlying readings twice — once as components, and again inside
CBBI's average of them.

**How to use it.** As the place you go to check my arithmetic — `#m4-score-5-reproduce-it-unaided`
sends you there. If CBBI's composite and the Score diverge, that is not one contradicting the
other; it is two different weightings of an overlapping set of measurements, and the interesting
question is which components account for the gap.

### Fear & Greed — sentiment, not an input

**What it is.** A 0–100 sentiment index built from volatility, momentum, volume, social activity,
dominance and trends.

**Why it carries zero weight.** It measures how people feel; the Score measures what the chain
and the price structure are doing. Sentiment is fast, noisy and mean-reverting over days, while
the Score is built for cycle position over months. Weighting one into the other would give a
multi-month instrument a short-horizon tremor.

**The specific evidence.** At the October 2025 top, **Fear & Greed never reached Extreme Greed.**
Anyone requiring a sentiment extreme to confirm that top was waiting for a confirmation that
never arrived. The readings are at liftoffr.com/indicator-history.

**How to use it.** As colour on a reading you already have. Never as the reading.

### Google Trends — attention, not an input

**What it is.** Relative search interest in Bitcoin over time.

**Why it carries zero weight.** It measures retail attention, which historically *follows* price
rather than leading it. Its scaling is also relative to whatever window you chose — a "100" in
one date range is not a "100" in another — so there is no stable level to weight.

**The specific evidence.** At the October 2025 top, Google Trends sat well below its 2021 levels.
That cycle topped without the retail attention spike the previous one produced.

**How to use it.** As a check on how crowded the trade feels, on a chart you read separately.

### Proprietary charting tools — not reproducible, so not eligible

Momentum and money-flow overlays sold as paid TradingView add-ons come up a lot. None of them is
in the Score, and the reason is structural rather than a judgement about quality.

**Every component of the Score reads from a free, public source**, so anyone can recompute the
number and check it. That is the promise the whole product rests on, and a component you cannot
independently pull would break it — you would be back to trusting me, which is precisely what the
published weights exist to make unnecessary.

There is a second reason. Tools of that kind fire on hourly and four-hourly timeframes. The Score
is built for cycle position over months. Even a good short-horizon signal would be answering a
different question from the one this number asks.

**How to use them.** However you like, on their own terms and their own timeframe. Just do not
expect them to agree with a multi-month cycle reading, and do not read a disagreement as either
one being broken.

interest, not a composite of its own inputs, and nothing you have to pay a third party to see.
