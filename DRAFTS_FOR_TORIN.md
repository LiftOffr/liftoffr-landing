# PART 1 — REVIEW REQUESTS

## Why this is worth doing before anything else

You have buyers. You have zero displayed reviews in a category where every competing
listing shows 4.8–5.0. That gap is doing active damage on `/plan` and `/system`, and the
guarantee blocks now shipped on both pages have space reserved for ratings the moment
they exist.

**One rule, non-negotiable:** never offer anything in exchange for a review, and never ask
only the happy ones. Both are FTC endorsement-guide problems (16 CFR 255) and both are why
the two unattributed testimonials had to come off `/playbook`. Ask everyone, take what
comes.

---

### Email A — to $29 plan buyers

**Subject:** `quick favour, and an honest question`

> Hey — you bought the Bear Market Buy Plan a little while back, so you're one of the few
> people who can actually answer this.
>
> **Did it do the job?** Not "was it nice" — did you actually open it, run the worksheet,
> and end up with levels written down that you'd follow?
>
> If yes: a review on Whop takes two minutes and does more for this than any ad I could
> run. There's no incentive attached and I'm not going to send you a discount for it — I'd
> rather have the honest one than the bought one.
>
> **If no, I'd genuinely rather hear that.** Reply and tell me what didn't land. If it
> wasn't worth the $29 to you, say so and I'll refund it, even outside the 30 days. I'd
> rather fix the document than keep selling a version that didn't work for you.
>
> [ Leave a review ] · [ Just reply to this email ]
>
> — Torin
>
> *Educational content only. Not financial advice.*

**Why this shape:** it asks the unhappy customers to speak too, which is what makes the
resulting reviews worth displaying — and it puts a refund offer in front of the people
most likely to leave a bad one, which is both the decent move and the one that protects
the rating.

### Where the reviews go once they exist

Both `/plan` and `/system` now carry full-size guarantee blocks with room directly beneath
for a rating. Display rules, so this doesn't recreate the problem that was just cleaned up:

- **Real name or verified initial, with permission on file.** No "— LiftOffr member".
- **No outcome claims in a displayed quote.** "Helped me write my exit down" is fine.
  "Made me $X" cannot be displayed, ever, even if true and even if the customer said it
  unprompted.
- **Show the count and the average, including the bad ones.** A 4.6 with 30 reviews
  outperforms a 5.0 with 3, and it's the honest number.

# PART 2 — RETROACTIVE CAPTION + PINNED COMMENT

## Read this first — it's the whole strategy

**You cannot fix the bottleneck by editing captions alone, and you should know that before
spending an evening on it.** The research is explicit: ~55% of reel views come from
non-followers who largely do not read captions, and Amy Porterfield's 85%
comment-to-registration came from *saying the CTA on camera and burning it in as text.*
That needs filming, and it's the ~38× lever. This is the second-best play.

**But it's free, it's retroactive, and it applies to every post you've already made.** The
pinned comment matters more than the caption — it sits directly under the video, it's
visible without a tap on "more", and it's the closest thing to an on-screen CTA you can
add to a post that's already live.

**One keyword per post.** `CYCLE` → the quiz. `SCORE` → the daily number + Sunday email.
Never both in one post. Run `CYCLE` on roughly 70%.

---

## The pinned comments — paste these directly

Pick by what the post is about. Pin one, don't stack them.

**P1 — default, highest priority (→ quiz)**
> Six questions and I'll tell you where you actually sit in this cycle — takes about a
> minute, no card, and you don't even need to give me an email to see the result.
> Comment **CYCLE** and I'll send it.

**P2 — for anything about selling, tops, or exits (→ quiz)**
> If you can't say in one sentence what would make you sell, you don't have a plan — you
> have a feeling. Comment **CYCLE** for the six-question version of the question.

**P3 — for anything about buying, dips, or fear (→ score)**
> The number I check every morning is public and free, no account needed to look at it.
> Comment **SCORE** and I'll send today's plus what it means.

**P4 — for anything personal / the 2022 story (→ quiz)**
> I had every indicator on the screen and did nothing, and it cost me about $30,000
> through 2022. Comment **CYCLE** for the six questions I wish someone had made me answer
> first.

**P5 — for anything where you're being skeptical of the industry (→ score)**
> I don't get paid by exchanges, I don't take affiliate money, and no token project has
> ever paid me. One free score, one $29 document, that's the business. Comment **SCORE**
> for the free part.

**P6 — the disqualifier, surprisingly strong (→ quiz)**
> This isn't for you if you want coin picks or something to trade this week. It's for
> people who own Bitcoin, have a job, and have never written down the price they'd sell
> at. If that's you — comment **CYCLE**.

## Caption openers — swap in as the first line

The first line is all most people see before "more". These are all hooks from the research
bank, cleared for compliance.

1. The most expensive thing I ever did with money was nothing.
2. Nobody loses money because they bought the wrong thing. They lose it because they never decided when to stop.
3. I had the winning trade on my screen for eight months and I still lost.
4. There's an entire industry telling you when to buy and almost nobody telling you when to sell. Ask yourself why.
5. Everyone thinks the hard part is picking. The hard part is quitting while you're ahead.
6. Answer honestly: if Bitcoin dropped 40% tomorrow, do you know what you'd do?
7. The four words that cost people more money than any bad investment: "I'll know when."
8. Why the smartest thing in my whole system is a rule that tells me to do nothing.
9. Show me a track record with no losing entries and I'll show you one you shouldn't trust.
10. I'm going to tell you not to buy my thing. Watch.

## Do these in this order

1. **Your top 10 posts by views.** Pin a comment on each. Highest return per minute of
   anything on this list — the views already happened and are still trickling.
2. **Anything that mentions selling, tops, or exits.** Best keyword-match to the quiz.
3. **Your pinned/profile grid posts.** Longest shelf life.
4. **Bio link** → `liftoffr.com/quiz` if it isn't already. Every retired magnet URL
   redirects there anyway, so nothing breaks either way.
5. **Everything else, opportunistically.**

**What to measure — and it isn't views.** It's **comments-per-1,000-views on the keyword.**
Currently implied at ~0.03–0.05%. First target 0.5%. Nothing downstream can be diagnosed
until that number moves, and if it does move, everything downstream has published
benchmarks saying it converts fine.
