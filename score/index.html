Corrected 20 Aug 2026

I found a bug in my own arithmetic on 20 August 2026 and shipped the fix the same day. This is exactly what changed, and why the number it replaced was wrong. When the data source publishes no reading for one of the nine on a given day, my code was treating that gap as a reading of zero and averaging it in, instead of leaving it out. On 19 August that understated the Score by 1.8 points — it read 34.5 when it should have read 36.3. (The 34.4 quoted further down is the same bug one day earlier, on 18 August, which is the date that recompute was run.) Anyone who pulled the data and recomputed it properly would have got a different number from mine and would have been right. A missing reading is now excluded and the divisor drops to match, which is what the table above shows. The 64-signal record at /receipts is unaffected — it was generated by a separate script that always handled gaps correctly.

Recompute it yourself

  1. Pull CBBI's public daily data. It is free and needs no account.
  2. Take today's value for each of the nine components above and scale it to 0–100.
  3. Multiply each by its weight, add them together, then divide by the weights you actually used — that is 1.00 on a normal day, and less if CBBI published no reading for one of the nine. A missing reading is left out, never counted as a zero.
  4. You should land on the number at the top of this page.

Want to check my working rather than your own? /api/cycle-score returns every component, its weight and its reading as JSON. Open it. An outside recompute on 18 August 2026 got 34.35 against a published 34.4 — and both of us were wrong by about 1.8 points, because we had each treated a missing reading as a reading of zero.
