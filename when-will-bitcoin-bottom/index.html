Three cycles is not a sample, and those last two rows are not a price target. Each of those bears happened in a different market: no ETFs, no institutional allocation, different halving dynamics, different rate environment. Every drawdown so far has been shallower than the one before it (84% → 83% → 76%), so applying an old percentage to a new top almost certainly understates the floor. LiftOffr does not publish price predictions; the rows are there because leaving the arithmetic out would be the dishonest version. Treat the whole table as a range of what has happened, never as a schedule of what will.

What actually moves at a bottom

Bottoms are not identified by a date. They are identified by a cluster of indicators reaching levels they only reach when everyone has given up. The nine components of the LiftOffr Score are live on the indicators page, each with what it read at every previous cycle bottom — that table is the honest version of this question.

The three that have historically moved first at a bottom are the slow, supply-side ones: the 2-Year MA Multiplier, Reserve Risk and NUPL. The momentum indicators confirm late, which is exactly the wrong order if you are trying to buy.
